Slick review pack

The rubric (FUTURE_WORK.md §2.3) — read before deciding
  1. Wind first. Below about 3 m/s there is no Bragg roughness for oil to damp, so the whole sea is dark and contrast means nothing; above about 10–12 m/s wind mixes oil down. Outside that band, defer. For these tiles the wind is unknown: the corpus records no acquisition time, so ERA5 cannot be looked up. Use the scene itself: if the sea around the instance is uniformly dark and featureless, defer. The tile's background (median band-2 backscatter outside every instance) is shown as a hint, not a wind speed.
  2. Linear, trailing a vessel, not widening — a bright target at one end, a straight or gently curving dark trail that stays roughly the same width → oos.
  3. Linear, diverging in a V from a bright target → a ship wake. Reject it. This is the most dangerous confusion: a wake is linear, dark, next to a vessel, and would be blamed on the ship that made it. "Target at an end" below is true for both; the V is what separates them.
  4. Irregular or compact, no vessel → slick_unknown.
  5. Sharp boundary and strong contrast → oil-like. Weak contrast argues a biogenic film → defer. Contrast is shown in dB (the instance against a ring of sea around it); it is relative, never a thickness.
  6. Never promote a Lookalike or No oil tile. This pack holds Oil tiles only.
  7. Uncertain? Defer. A deferral costs nothing; a wrong label trains the model to be wrong. Your name is recorded as <name> (non-specialist, rubric-assisted) unless you tick the analyst box, with your confidence.

Background: Brekke & Solberg 2005, Remote Sensing of Environment 95(1):1–13; Topouzelis 2013; EMSA CleanSeaNet guidance. Proposals below are the tool's suggestion from shape and bright-target distance only, never a label.
