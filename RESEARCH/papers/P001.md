## Content of use

### SAR physics and operational advantages (Section I.A)
- All-weather, day/night monitoring — unlike optical sensors
- Wide-area coverage in a single pass
- Detects thin oil layers, enabling early response
- **Mechanism:** oil smooths sea-surface roughness (damping capillary and short gravity
  waves), reducing radar backscatter, so slicks appear as **dark patches**
- Integrates with optical, thermal and AI-based models for accuracy

### SAR limitations
> "Low-wind areas or natural biogenic films are very likely mistaken for oil spills.
> Further validation is required through optical imagery, drones, or in-situ sampling."

Consistent with P002 (Jafarzadeh 2021) and P004 Section 3.6. Three independent sources now
agree the wind gate + optical cross-check is the standard remedy.

### AIS limitations (Section II) — the honest part
Two limitations stated plainly and worth carrying forward:
1. **Vessels can deliberately switch off their AIS transponders to avoid detection.**
2. **AIS provides no environmental or sea-surface information** — it can place a vessel but
   can never detect a spill.

Consequence: any attribution system must handle **dark vessels** (no AIS) as first-class
candidates, not as a gap in coverage. This corroborates Cerulean's dark-vessel branch.

### The five AIS methods for spill tracking (Section II.A)
Monitoring and real-time collection; anomaly detection for potential polluters;
cross-referencing AIS with remote sensing; **historical AIS analysis for source
attribution**; integrating AIS with oceanographic data for drift prediction.

Method 4 is a plain-language statement of our part (c), and method 5 of our part (b). The
paper describes both as things one *should* do; it does not do either.

### Model descriptions (Section III)
Textbook architecture walkthroughs of **Swin Transformer** (hierarchical stages, shifted
windows, patch merging), **U-Net** (572x572x3 input, 4 encoder/decoder blocks, skip
connections, 388x388x2 output, valid convolutions) and **DeepLabV3+** (atrous convolution,
ASPP with five parallel branches, encoder-decoder with low-level feature fusion).

These are accurate but generic reproductions of the original architecture papers. Their
closing recommendation:

> "For fast and lightweight segmentation, U-Net; for high accuracy and intricate oil spill
> structures, DeepLabV3+; for large-scale satellite imagery and advanced modeling, Swin
> Transformer."

This is unsupported by any experiment in the paper. **We follow P004's evidence-backed
YOLO-seg + LSK choice instead**, which additionally gives us the OOS/unknown-origin class
distinction that none of these three architectures provides on its own.

### Results section
Describes a Leaflet-based web prototype with a U-Net backend: satellite image upload,
confidence scores, vessel positions from AIS, ship-anomaly filtering by type and severity,
historical spill incidents, location search, base-map switching.

Useful only as a **UI feature checklist**. Our Phase 7 frontend covers all of this and adds
the time slider, backward particle cloud and evidence cards.

## Limitations of this source

- No experiments, no dataset, no metrics, no code, no ablations.
- Its architecture descriptions are copied from the originating papers.
- Its model recommendations are assertions, not findings.
- The reference list mixes plausible entries (Solberg 2007; Fingas and Brown 2014;
  Espedal 1999; Karantzalos and Argialas 2008; Fiscella 2000) with three suspiciously
  generic entries — Garcia and Thomas 2020; Lu and Zhao 2019; Manfreda and Tarantino 2021 —
  that were not independently verified. **Do not cite refs [1]-[3] from this paper without
  verifying them first.**

## Genuinely useful references it points to (classical SAR oil spill detection)

These are real, well-known works predating the deep-learning era, and they define the
classical baselines our detector should beat:

- Solberg, Brekke, Husoy 2007 — Oil spill detection in Radarsat and Envisat SAR images,
  *IEEE TGRS* 45, 746–755
- Solberg, Storvik, Solberg, Volden 1999 — Automatic detection of oil spills in ERS SAR
  images, *IEEE TGRS* 37, 1916–1924
- **Espedal 1999 — Satellite SAR oil spill detection using wind history information**,
  *Int. J. Remote Sens.* 20, 49–65 — *the foundational citation for the wind gate*
- Fiscella et al. 2000 — Oil spill detection using marine SAR images
- Karantzalos and Argialas 2008 — Automatic detection and tracking of oil spills in SAR
  imagery with level set segmentation
- Fingas and Brown 2014 — Review of oil spill remote sensing, *Mar. Pollut. Bull.* 83, 9–23
- Espedal and Johannessen 2000 — Detection of oil spills near offshore installations using SAR
