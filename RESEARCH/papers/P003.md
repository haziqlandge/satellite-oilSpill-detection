## Why it matters

Despite its weaknesses, this is the only source among the four that describes an
**end-to-end system of the same shape as ours** (AIS pipeline + SAR pipeline + fusion +
alerting + UI). Its value is threefold: a concrete AIS feature set, a synthetic-anomaly
generation recipe we can reuse for Indian waters, and — most usefully — **an architectural
mistake that clarifies our own contribution by contrast**.

## Approach as described

Two parallel pipelines joined at the end:

```
AIS -> preprocess -> feature extraction -> Isolation Forest / One-Class SVM -> anomalies
                                                                                  \
                                                                                   -> "Anomaly & Spill Correlation" -> Decision Support
                                                                                  /
SAR -> preprocess -> DeepLabV3+ (ResNet50 backbone, ASPP) -> segmentation ---------
```

Trigger logic (their Figure 1): AIS anomaly detected -> **trigger Sentinel-2 satellite
monitoring** -> if spill confirmed, alert authorities and nearby vessels.

## Usable content

### AIS feature set
`SOG` (speed over ground), `COG` (course over ground), `ROT` (rate of turn), `Heading`,
`Draught`, plus latitude, longitude and timestamp. This matches the marinecadastre schema
we will ingest and is a sensible starting feature list.

### Unsupervised anomaly baselines
**Isolation Forest** with anomaly score `S(x,n) = 2^(-E(h(x))/c(n))`, tuned over 100–500
trees; **One-Class SVM** with RBF kernel; percentile-based thresholding. These are the
right *class* of method for our problem because **labelled polluter behaviour does not
exist** — we can only model normal traffic and flag deviation. We should use them as an
unsupervised baseline component of `S_behaviour`, not as the whole scorer.

### Synthetic anomaly generation recipe (directly reusable)
- Inject noise into normal trajectories
- Simulate erratic movement, abrupt speed changes, irregular routes
- Generate additional anomalies via Gaussian distribution and time-series perturbation
- Label normal vs anomalous by deviation thresholds

This is exactly what our Indian-waters synthetic AIS generator needs. **We must avoid their
circularity**: our synthetic ground truth will be the *scripted discharge event we author*,
not the output of an anomaly detector.

### SAR augmentation set
Rotation (-30 to +30 degrees), horizontal/vertical flips, Gaussian noise injection,
adaptive histogram equalisation for contrast. Note P004 used only mirroring — rotation on
geocoded SAR must be applied before georeferencing is relied upon, or it invalidates the
pixel-to-geo mapping. **Use mirroring only for anything downstream of geocoding.**

### Training details
DeepLabV3+ at 256x256x3, ResNet50 backbone, Adam (beta1 0.9, beta2 0.999, eps 1e-8),
lr 1e-3 with validation-loss decay, batch 8–32, loss = categorical cross-entropy + Dice.
The **cross-entropy + Dice combination** is a sound choice for class-imbalanced segmentation
and worth carrying over.

## The architectural flaw — and why it matters to us

Their two pipelines run independently and are joined by a box labelled *"Anomaly & Spill
Correlation"* that is never specified beyond that name. There is:

- **no physical drift model** between the slick and the vessel;
- **no backward propagation** from slick to origin;
- **no notion that the slick has moved** since it was discharged.

Their correlation can therefore only ask *"was there an anomalous vessel near the slick's
current position?"* — but a slick observed at position X was released somewhere else,
possibly hours or days earlier. P004's Case 3 (source vessel berthed 2 days before
acquisition, track not matching slick shape) is a concrete instance this architecture
cannot solve.

They also invert the causal direction: their pipeline is **AIS anomaly triggers satellite
tasking**. That is a useful prevention posture, but it is the opposite of the NTRO problem
statement, which is **spill observed first, vessel identified afterwards** (forensic
attribution). Both directions are valuable; ours is the harder and the one specified.

> This flaw is the clearest statement of our contribution: **the drift model is the missing
> link between the two pipelines.** We insert `origin_field P(lat, lon, t)` exactly where
> their unspecified "correlation" box sits.

## Their own stated future work

Real-time weather/oceanographic fusion into AIS anomaly detection; larger labelled SAR
datasets; operational deployment with streaming; deep reinforcement learning for vessel
behaviour; blockchain for tamper-proof spill reporting; AUVs for submerged oil; multi-agency
satellite collaboration (Sentinel, NOAA, **ISRO**).

Of these, only **real-time met-ocean fusion** is in our scope — and we do it far more
directly, as physics rather than as extra ML features. Blockchain and AUVs are out of scope.
The ISRO mention is a useful pointer for the Indian-waters demo region.

## Engineering implications for our build

1. **Reuse** the AIS feature set (SOG, COG, ROT, Heading, Draught, lat/lon, time).
2. **Reuse** Isolation Forest / One-Class SVM as the unsupervised component of
   `S_behaviour`, with rule-based features alongside.
3. **Reuse** the synthetic-anomaly generation recipe for the Indian-waters generator — but
   author ground truth explicitly, never derive it from a detector.
4. **Reuse** cross-entropy + Dice loss for any semantic-segmentation component.
5. **Do not reuse** rotation augmentation on geocoded imagery.
6. **Do not cite** any of their metrics.
7. **Design against their flaw:** the drift model sits between the SAR and AIS pipelines.
   Our fusion is physical, not a correlation heuristic.
