## Why it matters

A recent, high-quality, peer-reviewed **review** covering the full lifecycle of AI in oil
spill management. Its value to us is not any single method but three things:

1. It settles the **build-vs-adopt question for the drift model** (adopt OpenDrift).
2. It supplies the **failure modes and open challenges** we must design against.
3. It gives a defensible framing of where our contribution sits in the field.

It is a review, so nothing in it is primary evidence — every claim is traced to an
underlying study, and should be cited that way.

## Scope of the review

Covers: AI-enabled risk prediction and failure forecasting; toxicological/ecological impact
assessment; oil spill fate and transport modelling (physics-informed methods, deep learning
trajectory prediction, uncertainty quantification); integrated remote sensing including
autonomous robots; intelligent manufacturing of remediation materials; AI-based decision
support systems.

## Findings that bind our design

### 1. Lagrangian particle models are correct; their weakness is cost, not physics

> Conventional Lagrangian particle-tracking models, such as **OpenDrift** and **MEDSLIK-II**,
> offer physically correct frameworks but struggle with computational complexity and
> real-time adaptation (Vasconcelos et al., 2025).

**Consequence:** use OpenDrift as the drift engine. Do **not** attempt to replace it with a
learned surrogate — the review nowhere claims a learned model beats the physics; it claims
the physics is slow. Slow is acceptable for forensic hindcasting, which is not real-time.

### 2. Ensembles, not single trajectories

Kampouris et al. (2021) investigated atmospheric forcing uncertainty through **ensemble
simulations coupled with MEDSLIK-II**, revealing substantial sensitivity to **wind phase
differences**, and enabling **probabilistic hazard assessments that communicate outcome
ranges rather than point predictions**.

**Consequence:** our backward run must be an ensemble over forcing and parameter
uncertainty, producing a probability field. A single backward trajectory would be
scientifically indefensible and operationally misleading. This is the direct justification
for the `origin_field P(lat, lon, t)` design.

### 3. Do not hand-roll turbulent diffusion

Nordam et al. (2019): random walk schemes commonly used for turbulent diffusion **require
careful implementation to avoid spurious accumulation artifacts** when eddy diffusivity
varies with depth.

**Consequence:** use OpenDrift's built-in vertical mixing. A naive Gaussian random walk
would create artificial particle pile-ups that our origin field would then misread as
high-confidence source locations — a silent, plausible-looking failure.

### 4. SAR alone cannot separate oil from natural films

Quoting Jafarzadeh et al. (2021): distinguishing oil contamination from natural surface
films using SAR imagery **alone is unreliable, particularly under low wind speed
conditions**.

**Consequence:** the wind gate is not optional polish. It is required for the detector's
output to mean anything. Reinforces P004 Section 3.6.

### 5. Bayesian optimisation of physical models works

Accarino et al. (2025) demonstrated an AI system using **Bayesian optimisation to tune
MEDSLIK-II hydrodynamic parameters in real time**; validated on the 2021 Banyas oil spill,
it improved trajectory prediction accuracy by **25%**.

**Consequence:** a credible stretch goal — calibrate our OpenDrift ensemble's wind-drift
factor and diffusivity against the observed slick shape rather than fixing them a priori.
Recorded as future work, not in the demo scope.

### 6. Detection-side AI state of the art (Table 2 of the review)

| Sensor | Problem addressed | Method | Study |
|---|---|---|---|
| SAR | Look-alikes, class imbalance, blurry boundaries | CNN encoder-decoder + **spatial attention** | Arnob et al. 2025 |
| SAR | Manual interpretation inefficiency, domain adaptation | **DeepLabv3+** semantic segmentation | Zakzouk et al. 2025 |
| Multispectral optical | Flow disturbance, vegetation shading (lakes); +3.3% mAP@50 | **YOLO-ADHF-SimAM** (YOLOv11 base) | Zhang et al. 2025c |
| Optical (Sentinel-2) | Transport-shaped slicks elongated by currents/vessel motion; separating slicks from **ship wakes** | Object-oriented fuzzy logic, multi-scale segmentation | Jiang et al. 2023 |
| Hyperspectral | Spectral instability, few labels | DAKD-SMS with **ViT** | Dong et al. 2025b |

Two of these corroborate our choices independently of P004: **attention mechanisms are the
consensus answer to look-alike confusion** (Arnob; and by extension P004's LSK), and
**YOLO-family + attention is an active, credible line for slick detection** (Zhang 2025c).
Jiang et al. 2023 is directly relevant to distinguishing slicks from **ship wakes** — the
exact error P004's baseline made in Fig. 4 Scene (a).

### 7. AIS is already used for AI-based risk prediction

Classification decision trees and deep learning are applied to AIS data for risk prediction
and prevention (Barberi et al. 2025; Murray and Perera 2021). The review also flags that
**AIS-based models may use commercially sensitive data, and data sharing between states may
be politically constrained**.

**Consequence:** relevant to an NTRO deployment. Our synthetic-AIS path for Indian waters is
not just a data-availability workaround — it is the sanctioned pattern when real AIS is
restricted.

## The seven open challenges (Section 5) — our design constraints

The review's own numbering; 1–3 apply across the whole lifecycle.

1. **Data scarcity.** Real spill datasets are fragmented and heterogeneous; confidentiality
   and inconsistent reporting limit robustness and generalisability.
2. **Regional generalisation failure.** Models trained in one region or on one oil type
   fail elsewhere due to differing hydrodynamics, sensor characteristics and weathering.
   *The review names generalisability as the **highest priority** of the seven.*
3. **Interpretability.** *"Many deep learning models operate as opaque black boxes, making
   outputs difficult for emergency personnel to evaluate, especially under high-stakes,
   time-critical conditions."* Explainable AI and uncertainty quantification are named as
   essential for stakeholder trust.
4. Decision support systems struggle to integrate multiple real-time streams under forecast
   uncertainty, sensor noise and data sparsity; coupling physical and data-driven models
   remains hard.
5. Real-time **data assimilation** is a capability physics-based models have and AI models
   largely do not; lack of standardised benchmarks blocks transferability assessment.
6. Autonomous robots are not yet mature (endurance, comms, swarm coordination).
7. Remediation materials do not scale from lab to product.

**Consequences for us:**
- **#2** justifies the **dual-region** design (GoM + Indian waters): we are explicitly
  testing cross-region transfer rather than reporting single-basin numbers.
- **#3** makes the **evidence card a hard requirement**. A ranked list of vessel names with
  a bare score is precisely the "opaque black box" the review says emergency personnel
  cannot act on. Every score must decompose into named, inspectable terms with the geometry
  that produced them.
- **#1** justifies the look-alike background samples and heavy augmentation.

## Limitations of this source

- It is a **review**: no original experiments, no released code or data.
- Published 2026 in a special issue; some cited works are 2025–2026 preprints whose results
  have not been independently reproduced.
- Table 1 (Mohammadiun et al. 2022) reports small-dataset response-model accuracies
  (Gaussian process regression: 93.49% train / 80.93% test) — the train/test gap is large
  and the dataset small; do not quote these as achievable targets.
- Coverage of AIS-based attribution specifically is thin — it is not the review's focus.
  P004 and the Cerulean system are far better sources for part (c).

## Engineering implications for our build

1. **Adopt OpenDrift**; do not build or learn a drift model. (Finding 1)
2. **Ensemble the backward run** over forcing and parameter uncertainty; output a
   probability field. (Finding 2)
3. **Use OpenDrift's own vertical mixing**; never a hand-rolled random walk. (Finding 3)
4. **Wind gate is mandatory**, not optional. (Finding 4)
5. **Evidence cards with per-term score decomposition** are a requirement, not polish.
   (Challenge 3)
6. **Report cross-region behaviour honestly**; do not present single-basin metrics as
   general. (Challenge 2)
7. Stretch goal: **Bayesian calibration** of drift parameters against observed slick shape.
   (Finding 5)
