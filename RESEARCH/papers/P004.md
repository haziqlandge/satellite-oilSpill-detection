## Why it matters

**This is the anchor paper.** It is the closest published work to the NTRO problem
statement: SAR-based automated oil spill detection with AIS-based source attribution.
Critically, its **Section 3.8 "Limitations and future work" reads almost verbatim as
parts (a) and (b) of our problem statement** — the authors explicitly name OpenDrift
reverse-trajectory hindcasting, instance segmentation for area/dispersion, and temporal
state classification as the things they did *not* do. Our project implements exactly that.

## Problem

Operational oil spills (OOSs) — illegal ship discharges and small platform leaks — are
continuous, small-volume, spatially and temporally uncertain, and largely unmonitored
(unlike accidental spills, which have mature response systems). Ivanov et al. 2018 found
that in the Barents Sea, transport and fishing vessels — not major accidents — are the
dominant source of marine oil pollution.

The methodological gap the paper identifies: **almost all existing detectors do binary
classification** (oil vs seawater). Binary output cannot support source tracking, because
it does not distinguish a linear vessel-associated discharge from an irregular slick of
unknown provenance.

## Approach

### Class scheme (the key design idea)

Two foreground classes; seawater is **background, not a class**:

1. `OOS` — operational oil spill: typically **linear**, associated with a vessel or platform
2. `oil slick of unknown origin` — more **irregular** shapes

Restricting to two foreground classes keeps the model focused on attributable targets and
makes the class label itself an attribution signal.

### Model: YOLOv8-LSK

Base YOLOv8 with three targeted modifications:

| Modification | Purpose | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| **LSK (Large Selective Kernel) attention module** | Dynamic large receptive field; captures the shape features separating linear OOSs from irregular slicks | Li et al. 2023 |
| **MPDIoU loss** replacing CIoU | Faster convergence, better localisation across scales; prevents overfitting on a small dataset | Ma and Xu 2023 |
| **SAHI (slicing-aided hyper-inference)** | Handles very large SAR scenes without destructive downscaling; adapts to varying input resolution | Akyon et al. 2022 |

### LSK placement ablation (L1–L5)

The paper's most reusable experimental contribution. LSK integrated at:

- **L1** — before the SPPF module
- **L2** — in front of the small-object head
- **L3** — in front of the medium-object head
- **L4** — in front of the large-object head
- **L5** — in front of **all** object heads

## Data

| Part | Content | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Part 1 | 80 Bohai Sea SAR images w/ spills (2022–2023) + 615 screened MKLab images | Build the OOS dataset |
| Part 2 | Bohai Sea, Jan–Mar 2021 | Evaluate detection across object types |
| Part 3 | Port of South Louisiana (TPSL), 2023 | Application case + AIS attribution |

- Sensor: **Sentinel-1 C-band SAR, IW mode, 250 km swath, 10 x 10 m, VV polarisation**
  (VV chosen per Garcia-Pineda et al. 2013 as best-suited to oil spill detection)
- Bohai images cut into **1024 x 1024** sub-images, giving 155 sub-images
- Combined with 615 MKLab images to form a **770-image OOS dataset**, split **8:1:1**
- Augmentation: horizontal + vertical mirroring
- **Look-alike images added as explicit background samples** (dark spots from environmental
  causes) to teach discrimination
- Final: train 2048 imgs (200 background), val 231 (25 background), test 231 (25 background)
- AIS source: `https://marinecadastre.gov/ais/` — vessel names, times, coordinates

### SAR pre-processing (SNAP)

Ordered chain, stated explicitly:

1. **Geometric correction** — aligns image to a geographic coordinate system. *The paper
   states this is what makes SAR/AIS matching possible.*
2. **Land masking** — prevents land being mistaken for oil
3. **Refined Lee filter** — speckle reduction preserving spill detail

## Training

RTX 4090. Default official YOLOv8 hyperparameters (deliberately — the contribution is
architectural, not tuning). `epochs=100, batch=32, imgsz=1024, lr=0.01, workers=8`.
LSK used the original paper's default parameters, held constant across all runs.

### Qualitative behaviour (Section 3.2) — decisive for model choice

- **Oil slicks of unknown origin (Fig. 3):** all three models handle clear-boundary,
  high-contrast slicks. L2 is more sensitive to small objects but emits redundant boxes and
  spurious tiny dark spots. Only L5 detected narrow, broken slicks in Scene (d).
- **OOS detection (Fig. 4):** baseline YOLOv8 and L2 **misidentified a ship's running wake
  as an OOS** (Scene a). In Scene (c), L2 misclassified an OOS as an unknown-origin slick;
  only L5 got it right. **L5 is the only model that reliably detects *and* classifies OOSs.**
- **Look-alikes (Fig. 5)** — sea ice, low-wind areas, waves, biogenic slicks, ship wakes,
  no oil present: baseline YOLOv8 produced **14 false positives**; L2 and L5 each produced
  **5**.

**Therefore L5 was selected as the final shipped model.** This is a qualitative decision,
not a numeric one — the averages tie with L2.

### Training stability (Section 3.4)

Baseline YOLOv8's `val/cls_loss` began at an extremely high value and collapsed almost
vertically — the signature of a model unable to separate the two classes (confirmed by the
Section 3.2 misclassifications). L5's `val/cls_loss` started low and descended smoothly,
and its mAP curve oscillated far less after plateau. Validation mAP50 0.89 to 0.92,
mAP50-95 0.66 to 0.69.

## Application cases — AIS attribution (Section 3.3)

All three at Port of South Louisiana, 2023. **AIS cross-checking was performed manually.**

### Case 1 — platform leak (Fig. 7)
Sentinel-1 C-band VV, **00:02 UTC 9 April 2023**. Narrow banded slick, ~5.5 km N–S.
Northern tip = suspected source at **29 06 58.176 N, 89 37 08.076 W**. Bright spots in SAR
matched an oil platform group on the electronic chart. **No ship trajectories within a
5 km radius** in AIS, so ship operation and accidental spill were excluded, confirming a
platform leak.

### Case 2 — moving-vessel illegal discharge (Fig. 8) *[our headline validation target]*
**00:02 UTC 15 May 2023**. Narrow strip, **~19 km long**, head/tail width visibly different.
Southern tip connected to suspected source at **28 21 31.968 N, 89 13 31.332 W**.
Exactly **one ship** in the target area coincided with a bright spot at acquisition time;
its trajectory matched the slick's shape. Vessel identified as **BOCHEM LONDON**.
Confirmed: OOS from illegal discharge by a moving ship.

### Case 3 — berthed-vessel discharge (Fig. 9)
**23:57:19 UTC 5 December 2023**. Three OOSs detected. One ~5 km slender N–S band, northern
peak = suspected source at **28 56 12.876 N, 89 58 07.356 W**. **The ship's trajectory did
NOT match the slick shape.** Vessel **BRANDON BORDELON** sailed SW to NE at 18:06:50 UTC
3 Dec, moored at 18:59:12 UTC 3 Dec, and was still moored when the satellite imaged the
ongoing OOS ~2 days later. Confirmed: illegal discharge by a **berthed** vessel. The other
two OOSs were attributed to platform leaks.

> **Engineering consequence:** Case 3 is the single most important case in the paper for
> our scorer. A naive "nearest moving vessel whose track parallels the slick" heuristic —
> essentially what Cerulean's parity + proximity terms compute — **fails here**. The true
> polluter was stationary for two days and its track does not align with the slick. Any
> scorer we build must still rank it above passing traffic.

## Limitations and stated future work (Section 3.8) — effectively our specification

1. **No temporal state granularity.** Current classes do not distinguish spill age or
   source proximity. Proposed future classes: (a) *ongoing discharges*, directly linked to a
   vessel; (b) *recent discharges*, slick detached but source vessel still nearby;
   (c) *legacy slicks*, source long departed.
2. **No automated traceability.** For slicks whose source has departed, the system
   "currently requires manual intervention to initiate tracking". Stated plan: automatically
   feed the spill's location and shape into an ocean drift model like **OpenDrift**
   (Dagestad et al., 2018) for **reverse-trajectory simulations**, replacing manual analysis.
3. **Bounding boxes only.** No precise contour means no accurate area and no dispersion
   monitoring. Stated plan: transition to an **instance segmentation** framework for area,
   volume estimation, and dispersion state.

## Look-alike elimination (Section 3.6)

Model optimisation reduces but **cannot eliminate** false detections, because some
look-alikes share oil's characteristics. The paper's recommended remedies:
- **Wind field information** significantly reduces or eliminates look-alike false detection
  (Liu et al. 2021)
- **Optical satellite observation** to check whether SAR dark patches are oil (Huang et al. 2022)

## Prior attribution work surveyed (Section 3.7)

| Work | Method | Automation |
|---|---|---|
| Liu et al. 2021 | AIS within 12 h of a long narrow slick; Analytic Hierarchy Process + expert scoring | manual scoring |
| Busler et al. 2015 | Probabilistic crossing of ship tracks with slick drift trajectories in space and time | semi |
| Luo et al. 2024 | Slick area converted to trajectory points; **bidirectional drift model** to find most likely ship | semi |
| Mizukoshi et al. 2019 | Human detection of discharging ships in SAR; AIS for identity | manual |
| Ivanov and Kucheiko 2014 | SAR + AIS + automatic ship detection; **manual** ship-info check | manual |

The paper claims it combines detection with source tracking and "solves the limitation of
high artificial dependency in previous methods" — though its own AIS correlation is still
manual per-case analysis. **This overstatement is itself the opening for our work.**

## Engineering implications for our build

1. Adopt the **two-class scheme** (`oos`, `slick_unknown`) verbatim.
2. Reproduce the **L1–L5 x {CIoU, MPDIoU} ablation** as our own evidence; ship **L5**.
3. Copy the dataset recipe including **look-alike background samples** — they are the
   mechanism behind the 14 to 5 false-positive reduction.
4. Fix the **SNAP chain**: geometric correction, land mask, Refined Lee. Geocoding
   fidelity is the precondition for every SAR/AIS overlay.
5. **Upgrade boxes to instance segmentation** (their future work #3) — required anyway to
   get area and to seed drift particles inside the true slick footprint.
6. **Add the OpenDrift backward run** (their future work #2) — the core of our problem
   statement's part (b), which the paper explicitly leaves undone.
7. Add a **wind gate** (Section 3.6) as a post-detection look-alike filter.
8. Use the **three TPSL cases as verification fixtures** — published ground truth with
   free real AIS from marinecadastre.
9. Treat **Case 3 as the adversarial test** of the attribution scorer.
